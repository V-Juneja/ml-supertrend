# ML Supertrend archived BUY-email replay

[Results and data](../analysis/results/archived_replay/) · [Replay source](../analysis/replay_archived_signals.py) · [Sensitivity table](../analysis/results/archived_replay/sensitivity.csv)

This test replays the archived daily BUY emails generated by the ML Supertrend. Each saved ranked list becomes eligible at the next session open under the execution rules below.

## Data and timing

The original archive contains 1,268 messages across 255 selected dates, 7 September 2025–14 October 2026. The first message for each date supplies ten ranked BUY candidates. The normalized ranking file contains only date, rank, symbol, score and signal; no email addresses, message bodies, receipt headers or account identifiers are published. The private receipt audit checks the original archive separately.

Replay period: **8 September 2025–30 October 2026**, 290 sessions. Signals become eligible at the first session open strictly after their signal date. Multiple signal dates mapping to one execution session use the latest eligible list. The last list carries forward through 30 October. There are no fresh archived signals after 14 October.

Prices: Alpaca SIP daily bars, split-adjusted. No raw market-price cache is distributed. The public normalized QQQ series and simulated equity/fill records are supporting outputs. Supply your own licensed bars to rerun the engine; provenance.json records input hashes. Dividends and cash interest are omitted. These are historical simulated executions, not broker-account returns.

## Execution rules

- Initial cash $100,000; $10,000 all-in cash debit per new name; up to ten holdings; no borrowing. Fractional shares reproduce the historical simulation.
- Rank order determines entries; only nonnegative-score BUY candidates qualify. No duplicate entry while held.
- Sell if the name is absent from the eligible list or the previous close is at/below 95% of its execution entry price or at/above 115%. Execute at the following open, with adverse slippage and fees. A name still on the list can be repurchased after an exit.
- Mark remaining holdings at daily closes. Ten remain open at the end of the baseline; no final liquidation is invented. The baseline applies 5 bps fees plus 5 bps slippage on each side.

All settings were held fixed across these tests; these are sensitivity cases, not a search for the best parameter set. The delayed case begins 9 September, one session later, and delays ranking-driven changes only. Stop and target exits still use the prior close and next open. Costs can change future holdings through available cash and entry-relative stop thresholds, so the return difference is not simply a subtraction of aggregate fees.

Higher costs reduce total return by 12.43 percentage points; a one-session ranking delay reduces it by 8.05 points. The recorded result changes materially with execution assumptions.

The standalone replay reproduced all **1,254 baseline fills** (632 BUY, 622 SELL) and all **290 daily equity marks** against the previously saved simulation. Dates, symbols, actions, shares, execution prices, fees, cash debits/credits and cash balances match within floating-point tolerance. This confirms that the published fill ledger and daily equity series reproduce from the saved rankings and execution rules.

## Trade expectancy

Net trade expectancy is the mean gain or loss per completed trade, expressed as a percentage of entry capital. Positive expectancy means gains exceeded losses and modeled costs across the historical sample. It is neither a win probability nor a guaranteed next-trade return.

**Net Trade Expectancy Equation:** `e = p W - (1-p) D`

Here `p` is the net win rate, `W` the mean net winning return, and `D` the positive magnitude of the mean net losing return. These inputs already include fees and slippage: costs must not be subtracted a second time. All return terms use percentage units.

| Core trade statistic | Historical result |
| --- | ---: |
| Completed trades | 622 |
| Win rate | 46.62% |
| Mean net profit / return per trade | +$81.73 / +0.8173% |
| Median trade return | -0.6268% |
| Average net winning / losing return | +8.05% / -5.50% |
| Profit factor | 1.28 |
| Break-even win rate at observed payoffs | 40.59% |
| Best / worst net trade return | +62.80% / -40.03% |
| Longest losing streak | 15 trades |

Average winners are 1.46 times average losers. The observed win rate therefore exceeds the **40.59% break-even win rate**, computed as `D / (W + D)`. Profit factor is the sum of positive net P&L divided by the absolute sum of negative net P&L: **$1.28 earned on winning trades for each $1 lost on losing trades**. The negative median means the middle-ranked trade lost money, while larger winners lifted the average above zero. The 15-loss streak and worst trade show the importance of sizing and gap risk.

All 622 completed trades are included; ten open positions are excluded. Streaks follow recorded SELL-ledger order, preserving same-day ordering. Returns divide net P&L by each trade's all-in entry cash debit. Average expectancy is distinct from cumulative account return because trades overlap, capital is reused and open holdings affect marked account equity.
